Research findings

Research finding

A new meta-analysis found that gaze-tracking tests identified autism in children with roughly 77% sensitivity and 80% specificity. Dynamic social videos and higher-quality cameras performed best. The results suggest possible value as one part of assessment—not a stand-alone diagnostic test—and variation between studies remains substantial.

Research finding

In a study of 63 young autistic children, sensory-seeking or repetitive sensory behavior was associated with greater executive-function difficulty across both caregiver reports and observations. The small sample and largely correlational design mean the findings cannot establish causation.

Source PubMed ↗
Research finding

A study of 2,722 predominantly late-diagnosed autistic people found that gender-diverse participants reported more co-occurring psychiatric diagnoses and more perceived prior misdiagnoses than cisgender participants. Because the sample was already weighted toward late diagnosis and relied partly on participant perceptions, the results may not generalize to all autistic people.

Source PubMed ↗
Research finding

Recent matched-sibling evidence continues to weaken claims that prenatal acetaminophen use causes autism or ADHD: associations seen in conventional observational comparisons moved toward null results when shared family and genetic factors were better controlled. Residual uncertainty remains inherent to observational research.

Policy & community

Policy & community

The U.S. Interagency Autism Coordinating Committee extended public comment on its draft 2026–2028 strategic plan until August 20 at 5 p.m. ET after advocacy organizations argued that the original four-day period was inadequate. The draft could shape federal priorities for research, services, communication access, and accountability. The extension is meaningful, although several organizations had requested 90 days.

Social-media pulse

Social-media pulse Discussion, not evidence

Recent public discussion is clustering around three tensions:

  • whether “neurodivergent” has become shorthand mainly for autism and ADHD, obscuring other conditions;
  • whether social-media content improves recognition for overlooked adults or encourages oversimplification and casual self-diagnosis;
  • how identity-affirming accounts of autism can coexist with the substantial disability and support needs experienced by other autistic people.

A 2026 analysis confirms the broader trend: autism and ADHD became increasingly central in Reddit mental-health discourse from 2015–2022. That study measures changing language and attention, not diagnostic prevalence or clinical truth.
