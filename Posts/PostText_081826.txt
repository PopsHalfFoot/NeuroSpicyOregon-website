One thing I genuinely love seeing today is how much more community there is for parents, families, and caregivers of autistic and neurodivergent kids.
Facebook groups. Parent networks. Advocacy organizations. Families sharing what worked, what didn't, and sometimes just saying, “Yep. We've been there too.”
That matters.
And I don't want to sound ungrateful for how far we've come, but the other day I caught myself doing that thing old people do…
“Back in my day…”
Oh God. I've become that guy.
But seriously—back in my day, it sometimes felt like there was a zero-tolerance policy for neurodivergent kids, especially in grade school.
If a child couldn't function the way the school expected them to, too often the question wasn't:
“What does this child need?”
It was:
“This child can't be here.”
My brother Ernie was kicked out of three grade schools before our family finally found one willing to work with an autistic child instead of treating his autism as a problem they needed to remove from the classroom.
Three schools.
And I remember what that did to our family.
There weren't Facebook groups full of parents swapping advice. There wasn't an online autistic community where you could hear directly from autistic adults. There weren't thousands of people one click away who could say, “We've been through this too.”
A lot of the time, families were figuring it out on their own.
And while we still have a hell of a long way to go, that's one of the things I think we should recognize and appreciate about where we are today.
Community matters.
Sometimes the most important thing another parent or caregiver can tell you isn't how to “fix” your kid.
It's:
“Your kid isn't the only one.”
“You're not the only family dealing with this.”
And maybe most importantly:
“There are people who understand.”
So yes, apparently I'm old enough to say “back in my day” now.
But I'm also old enough to remember why having this kind of community matters.
And I'm really damn glad it's here.
