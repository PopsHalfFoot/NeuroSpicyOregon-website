LET'S START WITH THE BASICS

Are neurodivergent and neurotypical brains actually different?

Neurotypical describes people whose development and brain function generally follow the patterns considered most common.

Neurotypical people may find it easier to:

  • Recognize and follow common social expectations
  • Adapt to standard routines and environments
  • Show a more even range of abilities on certain assessments

That doesn’t mean “normal,” better or perfect. It’s simply one valid way of experiencing the world.

Neurodivergent is an umbrella term for people whose brains develop or function differently from those typical patterns. It can include autism, ADHD, dyslexia, dyspraxia, dyscalculia, Tourette syndrome and other neurological differences.

Neurodivergent people sometimes have a spiky profile: unusually strong abilities in some areas alongside significant challenges in others.

But what is happening inside the brain?

Explaining science for awareness—not professional advice

Pops, the Science Guy translates research and scientific ideas into plain language for education and discussion. It is not medical, mental-health, diagnostic, legal, or other professional advice. Research changes, summaries leave out detail, and no finding applies to every person.
